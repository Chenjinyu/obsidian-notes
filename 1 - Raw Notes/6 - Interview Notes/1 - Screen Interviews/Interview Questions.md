# Interview Questions

A growing question bank for HR/recruiter screening interviews, with sample answers to practice aloud and notes explaining the strategy.

This note covers answers to salary-expectation questions and the compensation information to understand before negotiating. Personal compensation figures are represented by placeholders.

## Salary Expectations and Compensation

### Preparation before the call

Know the following before speaking with the recruiter:

| Item | Purpose | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private minimum | The lowest package worth accepting for this particular role | Use it to evaluate an offer; do not volunteer it at the start of the call. |
| Negotiation target | The compensation outcome to aim for | Explain it as a target with genuine flexibility, rather than a nonnegotiable requirement. |
| Employer's approved range | What may be possible for the role and level | Ask before committing to a specific expectation. |

**Keep the minimum and target separate.** The minimum helps decide whether to accept; the target guides what to request. Assess both against market information, the actual role, the package, and alternatives to taking the job.

Confirm the currency and whether the conversation concerns base salary or annual total compensation. A personal target is not itself evidence of the market rate.

### Q1. "What are your salary expectations?"

**Preferred first response:**

> I'm interested in the role. Could you share the budgeted base salary range and how bonus and equity(股权) fit into the package?

Then pause and let the recruiter answer.

**Why:** Understand the employer's budget before giving a number that could be unnecessarily low. The question may simply check whether expectations overlap; it does not prove the recruiter is trying to underpay the candidate.

**Clarify:**

- What level is the role being considered for?
- What currency are the figures in?
- Is the range **base salary** or annual **total compensation**?
- What are the target bonus and annual equity amounts?

### Q2. "We need your expectations first. What number are you targeting?"

**Answer to practice:**

> My target is around [target amount and currency] in annual compensation from base salary, target annual bonus, and equity vesting that year, excluding a one-time signing bonus. That's a target rather than a firm minimum. I'm open to discussing the role and overall package. Does that overlap with your budget?

**Short version:**

> I'm targeting around [target amount and currency] in annual compensation excluding a one-time signing bonus, with flexibility depending on the level and package. What range is budgeted for the role?

Replace the placeholders before practicing. Use this wording only when the flexibility is genuine. Do not describe the target as a hard minimum if a lower offer could be acceptable.

### Q3. Should I give one target or a range from my minimum to my target?

For a candidate trying to maximize compensation while remaining open to lower offers, a practical approach is to ask for the employer's range first, then state a flexible, defensible target if needed.

| Wording | What it communicates |
| --- | --- |
| "I require at least [target]." | A hard cutoff; inappropriate if less could be acceptable. |
| "My range is [private minimum] to [target]." | Immediately reveals that the private minimum could be acceptable. |
| "I'm targeting around [target], with flexibility depending on the role and package." | Keeps the goal clear while allowing discussion of lower budgets. |

A range is not inherently bad. Columbia research distinguishes ranges that extend below a starting request from ranges that include or extend above it; their outcomes differed. It does not show that recruiters always focus only on the lower endpoint.

Automatically announcing the entire acceptable range can reveal the minimum before learning what the employer can offer. A researched, role-specific range can still be appropriate when genuinely needed.

### Q4. "That is above our budget. Are you flexible?"

If the employer's maximum is below the target but the role remains worth considering:

> That's below my target, but I'm open to continuing the conversation. What determines where someone lands within that range, and is there flexibility based on the level?

Continuing the interview does not mean accepting an offer or receiving a promise that the employer will reach the target.

| Situation | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| The range can support the target | Continue and establish why the candidate's experience fits the level and responsibilities. |
| The firm maximum is below the target but above the minimum | Decide whether the role remains worthwhile; say honestly if continuing is acceptable. |
| The firm maximum is below the actual minimum | Clarify that the packages are comparable, then decline unless the threshold genuinely changes. |
| The budget or compensation components are unclear | Ask for details before deciding whether expectations overlap. |

If an advertised firm ceiling is acceptable, do not imply that a higher target is mandatory merely to preserve an opening position.

### Q5. Could a high expectation end the process during the phone screen?

**Yes. Possible responses include:**

- The recruiter shares the budget and asks whether the candidate is flexible.
- The recruiter continues because the target still overlaps with the approved range.
- The recruiter checks whether another level or package is possible.
- The recruiter stops the process or decides afterward not to advance the candidate.

A number above the employer's initial intended offer may still fit within its approved maximum. A number above a firm maximum is a different situation.

Calling the target flexible communicates room for discussion, but it does not guarantee continued consideration. Asking for the employer's range first provides better information for choosing how to respond.

### Q6. "What is the lowest compensation you would accept?"

Do not automatically disclose the threshold before understanding the role and package. One response is:

> I'd like to understand the full package before setting a firm minimum. My target is around [target amount and currency], and I'm open to discussing the right opportunity. What is the approved range for this level?

If the recruiter genuinely requires a concrete threshold to proceed, decide whether to disclose an honest number or end the discussion. No wording guarantees both maximum negotiating room and continued consideration.

Do not invent a hard minimum or falsely claim that an unacceptable package would work.

### Q7. Should I follow the advice "never answer salary-expectation questions"?

Treat it as a warning against quoting too low, not an absolute rule.

The concern is that a candidate may fear rejection and name a conservative number just to reach the next round, although the company could offer more.

A practical approach:

1. Research the specific role, location, and level.
2. Know the minimum and target before the call.
3. Ask for the employer's range first.
4. If needed, state a defensible target or range with honest flexibility.
5. Avoid lowering expectations solely out of fear of losing the next interview.

The sources below do not establish that most candidates quote below the employer's range, or that refusing to answer always produces a better offer.

### Q8. What should I negotiate during the first screen versus after an offer?

| Stage | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| HR/recruiter screen | Establish whether the role, level, location, and compensation could fit. |
| Later interviews | Understand the scope and demonstrate experience and impact relevant to the level. |
| Offer discussion, before accepting | Negotiate the concrete package using confirmed responsibilities and compensation details. |

Giving an expectation during the screen does not automatically prevent later negotiation. If the scope or level changes, explain why that changes the compensation discussion.

**Example offer-stage response:**

> I'm excited about the role. Given the responsibilities we've discussed, could we explore bringing the annual total compensation closer to [target amount and currency]? I'm open to discussing the mix of base salary, bonus, and equity.

This is a practice counteroffer, not a claim that every employer can meet the target.

## Compensation Structure: What I Need to Understand

A quoted "total compensation" figure is not enough on its own. Establish **what is included, which year it describes, what is fixed versus conditional, when it is paid, and whether it can become spendable cash**.

### Main components

| Component                             | Meaning                                                                              | What to establish                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Base salary                           | Fixed cash pay for the role, quoted on an annual or other stated basis               | Currency, gross amount, pay frequency, role level, and applicable salary band.                 |
| Target annual cash bonus              | An incentive(激励) amount used for planning; actual payout follows the employer's plan | Target percentage or amount, performance formula, eligibility, payout date, and any guarantee. |
| Equity                                | Share-based compensation, such as RSUs or employee stock options                     | Award type, number of units/shares, valuation basis, vesting, and ability to sell.             |
| Signing bonus                         | Joining-related cash or another award, sometimes paid in installments                | Amount, dates, duration, repayment conditions, and whether it is included in quoted TC.        |
| Equity refresher                      | A possible additional grant after joining                                            | Whether it is committed or discretionary; its own vesting schedule.                            |
| Benefits and retirement contributions | Employer-provided value beyond salary and incentive awards                           | Coverage, employer contributions, employee costs, eligibility, and waiting periods.            |
| Relocation or other joining support   | Assistance tied to starting or moving for a job                                      | What is covered, whether paid in advance or reimbursed, and repayment terms.                   |

**Annual bonus is different from signing bonus.** In the salary-answer scripts here, "bonus" means the target annual cash incentive, with signing payments discussed separately. An annual incentive opportunity may recur, but its payment is not automatically guaranteed.

**RSUs are different from options.** RSUs can deliver shares or cash after plan conditions are satisfied. Options give a right to buy shares at a specified exercise price within a defined period. Do not treat a headline option award as equivalent to cash or an RSU award of the same stated size. See the equity-plan references below.

### How to compare annual amounts

For this checklist, use these definitions and ask the employer whether theirs differs:

- **Annual package excluding signing payments:** base pay for the period + target annual cash bonus for that period + estimated value of equity scheduled to vest in that period.
- **Estimated first-year TC including signing payments:** the above components for the first year + signing payments scheduled for that year.
- Track relocation reimbursements, benefit value, and other allowances separately unless explicitly included, so they are not counted twice.

This is an **offer estimate**, not a guarantee of actual earnings. Bonus outcomes, share prices, eligibility, and vesting conditions can change the result. For options, request the valuation method separately instead of inserting the full underlying share value into the formula.

Do not count an entire multiyear equity grant as one year's compensation, assume equal annual vesting, or assume future refresher grants without a commitment. Compare the package year by year, including after the initial grant ends.

## Questions I Should Ask the Recruiter or Employer

Use the phone screen to establish broad alignment. The detailed questions can follow once there is mutual interest or a written offer. Not every question belongs in the first call, and some require the compensation, benefits, or stock-plan team.

### Four priority questions for the phone screen

1. **Level and range:** "What level is this role being considered for, and what is the budgeted base salary range for this location? Are the figures in CAD and before tax?"
2. **Components:** "How is the package divided between base salary, target annual bonus, and equity? Does the quoted total include a signing bonus or benefits?"
3. **Year-to-year package:** "Could you outline the first-year package and how it changes in later years as signing payments end and equity vests?"
4. **Flexibility:** "Which parts of the package can be adjusted based on experience, and is there flexibility in the level?"

If the role is outside Canada, replace CAD with the appropriate currency. If the recruiter cannot provide every detail yet, ask when the written breakdown will be available.

### A. Base salary, level, and location

**Understand:** The posted range may cover several locations or levels. Establish which band applies to the actual offer.

- "What is the annual base salary for this specific level and work location?"
- "What determines placement within the range, and is the final level decided before or after the interviews?"
- "Would working remotely, relocating, or transferring to another office change the compensation band?"
- "What is the pay frequency, and are there separate on-call, overtime, or other cash payments for this role?"

Do not assume additional hours receive additional pay; ask what the role's policy provides.

### B. Annual cash bonus

**Understand:** The target is a planning amount. Eligibility, performance results, and plan rules determine actual payout.

- "What is the target annual bonus, and is the percentage applied to annual base salary or eligible salary earned during the bonus period?"
- "Is the bonus based on individual, team, company performance, or a combination? What are the payout range and any cap?"
- "Is any amount guaranteed, including for the first year, or is the entire award discretionary?"
- "How does my start date affect eligibility or proration, when is the bonus paid, and must I still be employed on the payout date?"
- "Can you share recent typical payouts for comparable employees relative to target, if available?"

Past payouts provide context, not a promise about future results.

### C. Signing bonus

**Understand:** A signing award can improve early compensation without increasing the later annual package.

- "Is a signing bonus available, and is it separate from the annual performance bonus?"
- "Is it paid once or in installments, and what is scheduled for each year?"
- "Is the signing bonus included in the total-compensation figure you quoted?"
- "Are there repayment conditions? What events trigger repayment, over what period, and is repayment full or prorated?"
- "If repayment applies, how is the amount calculated, including gross versus net amounts and payroll adjustments?"

Request the written terms rather than assuming that every employer uses the same repayment rule.

### D. Equity type, valuation, vesting, and liquidity

**Understand:** The initial grant size, annual vesting amount, current estimated value, and eventual sale proceeds are different figures.

- "Is the award RSUs, stock options, performance-based shares, or another type? Is grant approval still required?"
- "Is the offer expressed as a dollar amount or a number of units? When and how is the dollar amount converted to units, and in what currency?"
- "What are the grant date, vesting start date, first vest date, vesting frequency, and any cliff or performance conditions?"
- "What is scheduled to vest in each year, rather than simply the total grant divided by the grant duration?"
- "Can vested shares be sold immediately, or are there trading windows, holding restrictions, settlement delays, or other limits?"
- "Are refresher grants committed or discretionary? When would I first be eligible, and how would those grants vest?"
- "What happens to unvested awards if I leave or the company ends my employment?"

If the award is **stock options**, also ask:

- "What are the exercise price, expiration date, exercise cost, and deadline to exercise after leaving?"
- "How was the quoted option value calculated, and what opportunities exist to sell the resulting shares?"

For a private company, ask specifically about liquidity and the share class/valuation used; an estimated value does not establish an opportunity to sell. Only apply these option/private-company questions when relevant to the offer.

### E. First-year versus later-year compensation

**Understand:** A large first-year total can depend on temporary signing payments, while an initial equity grant may vest unevenly or end later.

- "Could you provide a written breakdown for each of the first several years, including base, target bonus, equity vesting, and signing payments?"
- "Does 'year one' mean the first 12 months after joining, a calendar year, or a company fiscal year?"
- "What changes after the signing payments end and after the initial equity grant finishes vesting?"
- "Which future amounts are committed, and which assume future raises, bonuses, refreshers, or a particular share price?"

Do not assume future raises or refreshers will automatically replace an expiring award.

### F. Benefits, retirement contributions, and paid time off

**Understand:** Benefits can affect the value and out-of-pocket cost of the package, but they are not interchangeable with cash salary.

- "Could you share the benefits summary for employees in this location, including employee premiums, coverage limits, and dependent eligibility?"
- "What retirement contributions or matching are available, what employee contributions are required, and are there eligibility or vesting periods?"
- "When do health coverage and other benefits begin? Are there waiting periods?"
- "How much paid vacation, holiday, sick, and other leave is available, and how does the policy work in practice?"
- "Are employer benefit or retirement contributions included in your quoted TC, or listed separately?"

For Canada, ask about the actual employer retirement plan, which may include an RRSP or another arrangement; do not assume that a U.S. benefits description applies.

### G. Relocation, work arrangements, and starting costs

**Understand:** Joining support and work-location requirements can change the practical value of the offer.

- "Is relocation support separate from the signing bonus? What expenses are covered, and are payments advanced or reimbursed?"
- "Are there repayment conditions, approval requirements, or deadlines for relocation support?"
- "What are the required office location, in-office days, travel expectations, and any reimbursement policies?"
- "If applicable, does the company cover work-authorization processing or related professional fees?"

### H. Reviews, future grants, and negotiation flexibility

**Understand:** A possible future increase is different from a commitment in the offer.

- "When would I first be eligible for a salary review, annual bonus, promotion review, and equity refresher?"
- "Does my start date affect eligibility for the next review cycle?"
- "If base salary is fixed, is there flexibility in the initial equity grant, signing bonus, relocation support, or another component?"
- "If joining means forfeiting an existing bonus or unvested award, could the offer address that documented loss?"

Do not assume every component is negotiable. Ask which elements are controlled by role/level policy and which can be adjusted.

### I. Written terms and payment conditions

**Understand:** The breakdown and relevant plan documents should identify what is promised and what remains conditional.

- "Could you send the written offer, bonus terms, equity/grant information, benefits summary, and any signing or relocation repayment agreement?"
- "Which amounts are guaranteed in writing, and which remain subject to performance, approval, continued employment, or discretion?"
- "What happens to unpaid bonuses, unvested equity, and repayment obligations if employment ends?"
- "What is the offer deadline, and can I have time to review the complete package?"

If an amount or condition changes during negotiation, request the revised written terms before accepting.

### A simple request for the complete breakdown

> To compare the offer accurately, could you send a year-by-year breakdown of base salary, target annual cash bonus, equity vesting, and signing payments? I'd also like to understand which amounts are guaranteed, which are conditional, and whether any repayment terms apply.

For a cash-flow view, record payment and vesting dates separately from the annual offer estimate. An annual target bonus or estimated stock value is not monthly salary.

### Before deciding whether to accept

- [ ] The role, level, location, currency, and gross-pay basis are clear.
- [ ] The base, annual bonus, equity, and signing components are separated.
- [ ] The bonus target, eligibility, first-year proration, and payment conditions are understood.
- [ ] The equity type, grant/vesting terms, annual amounts, and ability to sell are understood.
- [ ] First-year and later-year totals are compared on the same basis.
- [ ] Future raises and refreshers are not counted as committed unless documented.
- [ ] Benefits, employee costs, retirement contributions, and relocation support are understood.
- [ ] Signing/relocation repayment terms and leaving-related conditions have been reviewed.
- [ ] All negotiated changes are reflected in the written offer or relevant agreement.
- [ ] The package meets the private minimum and is preferable to the alternatives.

## Quick Rehearsal

**Ask the budget first:**

> Could you share the compensation range budgeted for this role, including base salary, bonus, and equity?

**If a number is required:**

> I'm targeting around [target amount and currency] in annual compensation excluding a one-time signing bonus, with flexibility depending on the level and package.

**If the budget is lower but still worth considering:**

> That's below my target, but I'm open to continuing. What determines placement within your range?

**Reminder:** Keep the target clear, keep the minimum private initially, and be honest about flexibility.
