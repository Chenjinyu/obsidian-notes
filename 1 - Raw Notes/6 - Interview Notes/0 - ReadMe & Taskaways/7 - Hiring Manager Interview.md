# Content

## **What Hiring Manager cares about:**

```
Technical ability     → already validated by coding rounds
Hiring Manager wants  → will this person thrive on MY team?
```

## **Common Hiring Manager Questions:**

#### **About your background:**

```
- "Walk me through your resume"
- "Why are you leaving your current job?"
- "Why do you want to work here?"
- "What do you know about our company/product?"
- "Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?"
```

#### **About working style:**

```
- "Do you prefer working alone or in a team?"
- "How do you handle tight deadlines?"
- "How do you prioritize when you have multiple tasks?"
- "How do you handle ambiguous requirements?"
- "How do you keep up with new technologies?"
```

#### **About team dynamics:**

```
- "Tell me about a conflict with a coworker, how did you resolve it?"
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager"
- "How do you give/receive feedback?"
- "Tell me about a time you mentored someone"
- "How do you collaborate with non-technical people?"
```

#### **About failures and growth:**

```
- "Tell me about your biggest failure"
- "Tell me about a project that didn't go as planned"
- "What is your biggest weakness?"
- "What would your current manager say about you?"
```

#### **About the role:**

```
- "What excites you most about this role?"
- "What are you looking for in your next role?"
- "What kind of projects do you want to work on?"
- "What does success look like to you in this role?"
```

### **How to answer using STAR format:**

```
Situation  → set the context
Task       → what was your responsibility
Action     → what did YOU specifically do
Result     → what was the outcome, ideally with numbers

Example — "Tell me about a conflict with a coworker":

S: "At my last job, a senior engineer and I disagreed on 
    the architecture for a new microservice"
T: "I was responsible for the backend design and needed 
    to move forward without blocking the team"
A: "I scheduled a 1-on-1, listened to his concerns, 
    then presented data comparing both approaches.
    We agreed to prototype both and measure performance"
R: "My approach was 40% faster, we adopted it, and 
    our working relationship actually improved because 
    he appreciated the data-driven discussion"
```

## **How to tell if you match their culture:**

### **Ask these questions to the hiring manager:**

```
About team culture:
  "How would you describe the team culture?"
  "What does a typical day look like for this role?"
  "How does the team handle disagreements?"
  "How is feedback given on this team?"

About work style:
  "Is the team more collaborative or independent?"
  "How much autonomy do engineers have over technical decisions?"
  "How does the team balance speed vs quality?"
  "Is there on-call rotation? How is it structured?"

About growth:
  "How do you support career growth on your team?"
  "What does the promotion process look like?"
  "What happened to the last person in this role?"  ← very revealing question
  "What are the biggest challenges facing the team right now?"

About management style:
  "How do you prefer to communicate with your team?"
  "How involved are you in day-to-day technical decisions?"
  "How do you handle underperformance on the team?"
```


**Read between the lines — green and red flags:**

```
Green flags ✅:
  - manager speaks positively about the team
  - clear career growth path
  - team has low turnover
  - manager encourages questions and pushback
  - work/life balance is respected
  - failures are treated as learning opportunities
  - "we" language — "we solve problems together"

Red flags ❌:
  - vague answers about culture — "we work hard and play hard"
  - high turnover — "we're rebuilding the team"
  - manager dismisses work/life balance questions
  - "we move fast" without mentioning quality
  - can't explain what success looks like in the role
  - negative comments about previous employees
  - "I" language — manager takes all the credit
```

## **How to self-assess culture fit:**

```
Ask yourself after the interview:
  ✅ Did the manager listen or just talk?
  ✅ Did they answer your questions directly?
  ✅ Did you feel comfortable being yourself?
  ✅ Do their values align with yours?
  ✅ Did they seem genuinely excited about the work?
  ✅ Would you be happy reporting to this person daily?
```

---

**Key insight:** The hiring manager interview is **two-way** — they are evaluating you, but you are also evaluating them. The best candidates ask sharp questions and treat it as a conversation, not an interrogation. Your goal is to leave knowing clearly whether this team is somewhere you'd genuinely want to work.
